The 3-day attorney review period

When a licensed agent prepares the contract to sell a 1–4 family home in NJ, either side's attorney can review it and cancel it within three business days.

Last checked 2026-10-17

How it works

  • Counting: the 3 days start when the signed contract is delivered to both buyer and seller. Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays don't count.
  • Disapproval: if an attorney disapproves, notice must reach the broker(s) and the other party within the 3 days. Otherwise the contract becomes binding as written.
  • Notice method: the regulation's text lists certified mail, telegram or personal delivery. In Conley v. Guerrero (2017) the NJ Supreme Court held that fax, email, personal delivery or overnight mail with proof of delivery also work.
  • Extension: buyer and seller may agree in writing to extend the period.
  • Where it applies: contracts prepared by real estate licensees for 1–4 unit residential property (and vacant one-family lots) where the licensee earns a commission. The contract must carry the warning in large print at the top of page 1.

What sellers typically do during review

  • Have your attorney check price, deposit timing, inspection and mortgage contingencies, closing date and who pays which certificate fees.
  • Order your town's resale certificate and the smoke/CO certificate early. Many fee schedules jump when you apply with fewer than 10 business days to go.
  • Gather the Seller's Property Condition Disclosure Statement (with the mandatory flood questions), survey, elevation certificate if you have one, and solar lease/PPA documents if any.

Practical tips, not legal requirements. Ask your attorney.

Sources

  • N.J.A.C. 11:5-6.2 (attorney review clause), via Cornell LII
  • Conley v. Guerrero (N.J. Supreme Court, 2017), opinion (PDF)
