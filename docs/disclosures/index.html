NJ seller disclosure requirements

New Jersey sellers fill out the Division of Consumer Affairs' Seller's Property Condition Disclosure Statement. Since March 20, 2024 its Flood Risk questions are mandatory under P.L.2023, c.93.

Last checked 2026-10-17

Flood risk disclosure (P.L.2023, c.93)

  • Applies to all sellers of real property, residential and non-residential. Questions 109–117 (the Flood Risk addendum) are mandatory for everyone; questions 1–108 are mandatory for residential sales.
  • Must be given before the buyer becomes obligated under the contract.
  • Asks whether the property is in FEMA's Special Flood Hazard Area ("100-year") or Moderate Risk area ("500-year"); whether federal law requires flood insurance; past flood damage, claims and FEMA/other assistance; and whether a FEMA elevation certificate exists (if so, it must be shared with the buyer).
  • Questions about flood history are answered from the seller's actual knowledge. Check zones with NJDEP's address look-up tool or FEMA's Flood Map Service Center.

Sources: P.L.2023, c.93 (flood risk disclosure law) · NJ DCA announcement: new disclosure forms required starting March 20, 2024 · NJDEP Flood Risk Disclosure FAQ (Sept 2024, PDF) · NJDEP Flood Disclosure Law home page

Other disclosure rules sellers hit

  • Solar panels (P.L.2023, c.312): for a single-family home with solar panels, the disclosure statement, contract or a rider must name the installer and the panel owner/PPA company. If panels are leased or under a PPA, it must state clearly whether the lease/PPA transfers and attach the surviving contract documents. Applies to panels installed on or after Jan 16, 2024, once the Real Estate Commission posted its guidance.
  • Lead paint (federal): for homes built before 1978, sellers must disclose known lead-based paint and hazards, hand over records, give the EPA pamphlet, include the lead warning statement and give buyers a 10-day chance to test. See the lead paint page.
  • Secondary power sources: homes with a generator, solar or battery need a warning label at the panel and meter before a change of occupancy. See the smoke/CO certificate page.

Sources: P.L.2023, c.312 (solar panel disclosure, N.J.S.A. 46:3C-13) · Division of Consumer Affairs: Seller's Property Condition Disclosure Statement (PDF) · US EPA: Real estate disclosures about potential lead hazards
