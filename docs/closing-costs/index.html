NJ seller closing costs

In New Jersey the seller pays the state Realty Transfer Fee. Since July 10, 2025 the seller also pays the Graduated Percent Fee (the old "mansion tax") on sales over $1 million. Nonresident sellers usually make an estimated income-tax payment at closing.

Who gets the reduced rate

Partial exemptions apply to qualifying senior citizens 62 or older, qualifying blind persons, qualifying disabled persons, and low- and moderate-income housing. The seller claims it on a notarized Form RTF-1 (Affidavit of Consideration for Use by Seller), recorded with the deed.

Full exemptions (deeds the RTF does not apply to)

  • Consideration under $100
  • To/from the US, the State or its agencies/subdivisions
  • Only to provide or release security for a debt
  • Confirming or correcting a prior recorded deed
  • Sale for delinquent taxes or assessments
  • On partition
  • By a receiver, bankruptcy trustee or assignee for creditors
  • Between husband and wife, or parent and child
  • Cemetery lot or plot
  • Executor/administrator to a devisee or heir under a will or intestacy
  • Within 90 days after a divorce decree, between the former spouses
  • Specific performance of a final judgment; release of a right of reversion; and others listed by the Division

Sources: NJ Division of Taxation: Realty Transfer Fee (rates, exemptions, Graduated Percent Fee) · NJ Division of Taxation: RTF Frequently Asked Questions

2. The "mansion tax" is now the Graduated Percent Fee, and the seller pays it

What changed in 2025: P.L.2025, c.69 (approved June 30, 2025; effective July 10, 2025) replaced the flat 1% buyer-paid fee on sales over $1 million with a graduated fee paid by the seller (grantor). The rate applies to the entire price, not just the amount over a threshold.

$3,500,000no limit3.5% of the entire price

It applies to deeds for property classed as Class 2 residential (1–4 units), Class 3A farm (only if it has a building suited for residential use), Class 4A commercial and Class 4C co-op units. Form RTF-1EE must accompany every deed over $1,000,000. The parties can negotiate who bears the cost in the contract, but the law puts it on the seller.

Transition rule: for contracts signed before July 10, 2025 with deeds recorded by November 15, 2025, the seller could claim a refund of the amount above 1% on Form RTF-3. The Division also notes that refund claims generally must be filed within 90 days of payment (N.J.S.A. 46:15-7.2(c)), so check timing with your attorney.

Sources: P.L.2025, c.69 (A5804), approved June 30, 2025 · Division of Taxation memo: Amendments to Graduated Percent Fee (July 2, 2025) · Division of Taxation: Graduated Percent Fee Exemptions · NJ Division of Taxation: Realty Transfer Fee (rates, exemptions, Graduated Percent Fee). Note: an older answer in the Division's RTF FAQ still describes the pre-2025 "buyers must remit the 1% fee" rule. The 2025 statute and memo control.

3. Nonresident sellers: GIT/REP forms

Every seller files one GIT/REP form with the deed. NJ residents certify residency or an exemption on GIT/REP-3. A nonresident individual, estate or trust generally completes GIT/REP-1 and makes an estimated Gross Income Tax payment at or before closing, whether or not there is a gain.

  • Amount: the gain × the top NJ rate (10.75%), but never less than 2% of the consideration (the seller's share). 2% is a floor, not a cap.
  • Alternatives: an exemption claimed on GIT/REP-3, a waiver on GIT/REP-4, or a prepayment receipt (GIT/REP-2). The forms list the qualifying situations.
  • If GIT/REP-1 is incomplete, the county clerk will not record the deed. Any overpayment is reconciled when the seller files an NJ nonresident return.

Sources: Division of Taxation: FAQs on GIT/REP forms · Form GIT/REP-1 Nonresident Seller's Tax Declaration (PDF) · Technical Bulletin TB-57(R): Estimated GIT payments by nonresident sellers (PDF) · Division of Taxation: GIT/REP forms list

4. Other common seller costs (not set by state law)

  • Mortgage payoff and lien releases; prorated property taxes and water/sewer through closing.
  • Real estate commission (negotiable), attorney fees, municipal certificate fees (see your town page) and smoke/CO certificate fees.
  • Federal capital-gains exclusion on a main home: up to $250,000 ($500,000 for most joint filers) if ownership/use tests are met. See IRS Topic 701: Sale of your home.
