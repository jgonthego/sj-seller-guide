Lead paint rules when selling

For a sale, the main lead rule is federal: disclosure for homes built before 1978. New Jersey's 2021 lead inspection law targets rentals, which matters if you rent out a property or are selling a rental.

Last checked 2026-10-17

Federal disclosure (homes built before 1978)

  • Disclose any known lead-based paint or hazards and give the buyer available records and reports.
  • Give the buyer the EPA pamphlet Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home.
  • Include the Lead Warning Statement and signed disclosure in the contract.
  • Give the buyer a 10-day opportunity to test for lead (they can waive it or agree to a different period).
  • Keep the signed acknowledgment for 3 years. Sellers aren't required to test or remove lead.

Source: US EPA: Real estate disclosures about potential lead hazards

NJ lead-safe certification (rentals): P.L.2021, c.182

  • Applies to most pre-1978 rental dwellings. It does not apply to owner-occupied homes being sold.
  • Municipalities must inspect for lead paint hazards at tenant turnover or within 2 years of the law's effective date (by July 22, 2024), then every 3 years or at turnover, whichever is first.
  • Exempt: units built in 1978 or later, units certified lead-free, seasonal rentals under 6 months, and some others listed by DCA.
  • If you're selling a rental, confirm its lead-safe certificate status with the town and your attorney.

Source: NJ DCA: Lead-safe certification FAQ (P.L.2021, c.182)
