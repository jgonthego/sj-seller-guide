The smoke alarm / CO alarm certificate

Before any 1- or 2-family home changes occupancy (a sale or a new tenant), New Jersey requires a certificate showing working smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms. Since February 3, 2025 the inspection also covers a secondary power source label.

Last checked 2026-10-17

Big 2025 change: fire extinguishers are no longer part of it

P.L.2025, c.19 (approved and effective February 3, 2025) amended N.J.S.A. 52:27D-198.1. It dropped the portable fire extinguisher from the resale certificate and added a check for a secondary power source identification label (for homes with a generator, solar PV, battery storage, etc.) located within 18 inches of the electrical panel and meter. The certificate formerly known as the "CSDCMAFEC" (Certificate of Smoke Detector, Carbon Monoxide Alarm and Fire Extinguisher Compliance) is now the CSACMASPSIL (Certificate of Smoke Alarm, Carbon Monoxide Alarm, and Secondary Power Source Identification Label Compliance).

The Division of Fire Safety told local enforcing agencies to stop enforcing the extinguisher requirement for these certificates. Some older town web pages and the codified regulation text (N.J.A.C. 5:70) still mention extinguishers until they are updated. The law also requires inspections to be done by certified fire inspectors; housing inspectors may keep doing them until Feb 3, 2027.

Sources: P.L.2025, c.19 (A3894), approved Feb 3, 2025: smoke alarms, secondary power source label, fire inspector licensing · DCA Division of Fire Safety memo: Secondary Power Source Identification Label (Feb 13, 2025, PDF) · DCA advisory bulletin: CSACMASPSIL / municipal COs and fire inspector licensure (Jan 13, 2026, PDF) · NJ Fire Safety Commission minutes, March 2025: removal of fire extinguishers discussed (PDF)

What gets checked

  • Smoke alarms on each level, outside each separate sleeping area, and as required by the rule for the home's age. Some homes need 10-year sealed-battery alarms when battery-only units are replaced.
  • Carbon monoxide alarms in the vicinity of sleeping areas if the home has a fuel-burning appliance, fireplace or attached garage.
  • Secondary power source label at the panel and meter, if the home has one.

Rule text: N.J.A.C. 5:70-4.19 (alarm placement), via Cornell LII · N.J.A.C. 5:70-2.3 (certificate on change of occupancy), via Cornell LII

Who issues it and what it costs

  • Issued by the town's local fire enforcing agency (fire bureau or fire marshal). Where the town has no local agency, the state Division of Fire Safety issues it. Where the town runs its own resale/CO inspection, the town may check alarms as part of that.
  • Fees in the state rule (where the state or a town without its own fee schedule issues it): $45 if requested more than 10 business days before the change of occupancy, $90 for 4–10 business days, $161 for fewer than 4. Towns that enforce locally can set their own fees by ordinance. See your town page.
  • Typically valid for 6 months. Selling without one can bring a penalty of up to $500 under the rule.

Sources: N.J.A.C. 5:70-2.9(d) (state certificate fees), via Cornell LII · NJ DCA Fire Safety portal: apply for smoke certification (where the State is the enforcing agency)
