Planning

Typical NJ home-sale timeline

Most financed sales in South Jersey close about 30–60 days after the contract is signed. Here's the usual order of events from the seller's side. Your contract's dates control.

  1. 1

    Before listing (2–6 weeks out)

    Pull your deed, survey, mortgage payoff estimate and any elevation certificate. Look up your town's resale certificate rules and the fee. Fix obvious smoke/CO alarm issues. Start the Seller's Property Condition Disclosure (flood questions are mandatory).

  2. 2

    Listing & offers

    Give the buyer the disclosure statement before they're bound by a contract. Add the federal lead disclosure for pre-1978 homes.

  3. 3

    Contract signed → attorney review (3 business days)

    Attorneys review the contract. Either side can cancel during this window. See attorney review.

  4. 4

    Inspections & mortgage (about 2–6 weeks)

    The buyer's home inspection, any repair negotiation, appraisal and mortgage commitment. Typical NJ contracts set deadlines for each.

  5. 5

    Apply for town certificates (as early as allowed)

    File your town's resale CO/inspection application and the smoke/CO certificate request. Apply more than 10 business days ahead to avoid rush fees. Allow time for re-inspection.

  6. 6

    Title, payoff & final readings (1–2 weeks out)

    The title company clears liens and orders the municipal lien search. Schedule final water/sewer readings if your town requires them. Nonresidents prepare GIT/REP-1.

  7. 7

    Closing

    Sign the deed and the RTF-1 or RTF-1EE if applicable. The transfer fee and GIT/REP payment are collected and the deed is recorded with the county clerk.

General planning outline, not a legal requirement. Deadlines come from your contract and your town.
